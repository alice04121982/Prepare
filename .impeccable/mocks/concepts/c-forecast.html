If the power went off, the water stopped and the shops were shut for three days, would you be all right?

The government asks every UK household to be able to manage on its own for three days. This site makes that practical: what to keep, how much, how long it lasts, and what to do first.

What might stop

Severe weather, a fault in the grid, a cyber attack on a water company, or disruption from a conflict elsewhere in Europe can all have the same effect at home.

  1. Heating

    The heating goes off.

    Helps: blankets, warm layers, a hot-water bottle.

  2. Water

    Nothing comes out of the taps.

    Helps: 3 litres of water per person per day.

  3. Shops

    The shops run short.

    Helps: tins and dry food you already eat.

  4. Payments

    Cards and cash machines stop.

    Helps: some cash in small notes.

  5. Networks

    Mobile networks and the internet go down.

    Helps: a wind-up or battery radio.

  6. Transport

    Buses and trains stop.

    Helps: knowing who nearby you can reach on foot.

  7. Medicine

    Prescriptions are hard to get.

    Helps: a week's spare medication where your GP allows.

Outlook

Most of these last hours or days, not weeks. All of them are easier with a few things in the cupboard and a plan you made while everything worked.

How much for your household?

18L

2 adults for 3 days need 18 litres of drinking water.

Why three days?

Since 2024

Since 2024 the UK, the EU, Sweden, Finland, Norway and France have all asked their citizens to keep a few days of supplies at home. None of them say anything is imminent. All of them have decided it is no longer sensible to assume it cannot happen.

Honestly

For the very worst cases there is little any household can do. For everything short of that, three days of supplies make a real difference.
