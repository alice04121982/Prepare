# Competitor review: prepping sites

Reviewed 18 September 2026. Two sites read in full: TruePrepper and The
Prepared. Both are US, both are well established, and between them they
cover the two ends of the market Stay Prepared sits between.

## TruePrepper (trueprepper.com)

**Who.** Sean Gold, US Air Force veteran (2007 to 2013), HazMat technician,
emergency management degree. Started 2016 "as a blogging experiment". Team
is family and friends in North Carolina, mostly ex-military, unnamed "for
the sake of OPSEC".

**What it offers.** Beginner guides, risk-assessment articles, gear reviews
(water filters, packs, freeze dryers, power stations), and checklists for
home supplies, bug-out bags, get-home bags and everyday carry. Food and
water storage guides. A deals section. A newsletter ("0% spam, 100%
prepping").

**Tone.** Measured for the genre. "Prepping is actually pretty reasonable."
Mission is "to spread and normalize prepping". No countdown clocks or
end-times language on the home page. But the identity is still prepper:
bug-out bags, OPSEC, firearms expertise on the team page, everyday carry.
The frame is the individual or family getting themselves through. Community
and neighbours barely appear.

**How it makes money.** Affiliate links throughout ("when you buy through
our links, we may earn a commission"), mostly Amazon. Exclusive partner
discount codes (for example TRUEPREP at Eberlestock). A curated deals page.
The newsletter feeds all of the above. It says it does not run sponsored
posts and has no pop-ups. Credibility comes from military service, hands-on
testing, and years of output.

## The Prepared (theprepared.com)

**What it offers.** Broad guides (basics, gear, first aid, bug-out bags,
home, self-defence, scenario planning for earthquakes, hurricanes, winter),
plus paid online courses (Austere First Aid, Water Essentials) and
ready-made kits.

**Tone.** Explicitly mainstream and rational. "The most trusted resource and
largest community of modern, rational people." "No politics or other
craziness. Everyone is welcome." It does have community sections (sharing
knowledge, CERT training, building groups), so it is closer to Stay
Prepared's stance than TruePrepper, though community still sits alongside
gear rather than above it.

**How it makes money.** Courses, kits, a membership tier, and affiliate
links in gear reviews. Credibility through named expert vetting ("medical
experts with 180 years of experience") and scale claims ("reviewed 100's of
bags").

## What both have in common

- Gear-led. The unit of content is the product review or the packing list.
- US-centric: FEMA, hurricanes, firearms, bug-out. Little of it maps to a
  British flat.
- Exhaustive rather than prioritised. Long lists that assume a garage.
- The imagined reader is one capable adult getting their household through.
- Revenue is affiliate commission, so there is always a quiet pull towards
  recommending more things.
- Neither says plainly what preparation cannot do.

## Where Stay Prepared is different

1. **Scenario-led, not gear-led.** Start from what actually happens (a power
   cut, a water notice, thin shelves, a storm, a long winter of rolling
   outages) and derive the short list from that.
2. **Community is the first layer, not a sidebar.** Two names before three
   days. The Ukraine evidence is the spine of this argument.
3. **Honest about limits.** For the worst cases there is little a household
   can do, and the site says so. The point is control over the part that is
   yours, not a fantasy of control over everything.
4. **UK-first.** 105, Priority Services Register, Met Office, gov.uk flood
   warnings, a small flat with no garage.
5. **Prioritised and finite.** A "starting from nothing" list of nine
   things, not a hundred.
6. **No bravado.** No OPSEC, no bug-out, no firearms, no everyday carry, no
   veteran framing. Plain language for people who feel helpless watching
   the news.
7. **Offline by design.** The single-file guide that works with no internet.

## Monetisation options for Stay Prepared

Current stance (PLAN.md): product links go to the maker's site, no
affiliate links, no retailer links, only gear actually used.

| Option | Income | Fit with the tone | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| No monetisation | None | Best | Costs are near zero on free hosting. Sustainable as a side project. |
| Maker-site links only (current) | None | Best | Useful to readers, no pull towards over-recommending. |
| Affiliate links with a plain disclosure | Modest | Tension | Every competitor does this. Doable honestly if the product list stays short and the disclosure is blunt, but it changes the incentive. |
| Donations (Ko-fi, Open Collective) | Small | Good | Common for mutual-aid-adjacent projects. Says "we are not selling you anything". |
| Printed or PDF guide at cost | Small | Good | The offline guide already exists. A printed A5 version at cost or small margin fits. |
| Courses or kits | Larger | Poor | Turns the site into a shop. Not recommended. |

Recommendation: stay non-commercial for launch, keep maker-site links,
add an optional donation link once there is traffic, and revisit affiliate
only if hosting or time costs make it necessary, with the product list
capped and the disclosure on every page that carries a link.
