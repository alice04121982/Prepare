# Copy audit, 19 September 2026

Every page read against two references: prepare.campaign.gov.uk (plain,
second person, says who does what and what you should do, never wry) and
Sweden's If Crisis or War Comes ("help will be provided first to those who
need it most; the majority must be prepared to cope on their own for some
time; the better prepared you are, the greater the opportunity you will
also have to help others").

## The test applied to each line

1. Would gov.uk say it? If it is a joke, a dig at other sites, or a
   comment on the site itself, it fails.
2. Does it put the household first and the community second, as a
   consequence rather than a slogan?
3. Does it say who is responsible for what: the household for a few days,
   the council, utilities and government for the fix and for help?
4. Is the reader told what to do, in the second person, without a
   flourish at the end of the paragraph?

## Kept, deliberately

- The Swedish-style list of what stops, and "First you and yours. Then
  everyone else." Both are the references speaking.
- The worried page's warmer register. It is talking to someone frightened
  and borrows from counsellors rather than from a government site; the
  tone guide allows this on that page only.
- "The most effective preparation is other people" survives nowhere.
